Cadence

An AI payment co-pilot for micro-agencies. It reads your client contracts, remembers the terms, and when a payment comes due it gives you advice and drafts the right follow-up email — in the right tone.

Audience: 1–5 person agencies Stack: Next.js · FastAPI · Postgres · Claude Deadline: Aug 25
01

The one thing to understand

Every other tool treats the contract and the invoice as two separate things. An accounting app sends "you're overdue" without knowing what was signed. Cadence connects them: it remembers what the contract said, and uses that memory to give advice and write a smart email when payment is due.

The whole product is one loop, repeated per client:

Cadence reads it and remembers the payment terms.

Why it matters → If a teammate only remembers one sentence, make it this: Cadence remembers the contract, so the reminder can quote what the client actually agreed to.

12

UX rules for the team

  • Confirm before automating. Whenever Cadence extracts something (contract terms, invoice fields), show it and ask "is this right?" before locking it in. One wrong number caught early beats a bad email later.
  • Always show the evidence. The advice card names what it read ("Invoice #001 paid on time", "email Sept 7"). Never a black-box verdict — reasoning you can see is what makes it trustworthy.
  • Keep "not legal advice" visible anywhere the contract is analyzed. Soften flags: "worth a second look", never "this clause is predatory".
  • Sentence case, plain verbs. Buttons say what happens: "Send", "Copy", "Create client". The button that says "Send" produces a message that says "Sent".
  • The user is always in control. Cadence drafts; the human sends. Copy / Edit / Send — never auto-fire an email.
  • Empty states invite action. A client with no contract yet says "Drop the contract PDF here to get started", not "No data".
The through-line → Cadence does the thinking; the user keeps the decision. Every screen should make the person feel informed and in charge, never managed by a black box.
